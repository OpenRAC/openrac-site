Something nice has been happening lately: **more and more people are working on Ratchet & Clank.** New
repositories, new faces on Discord, new pull requests from people we had never met.

We think that is wonderful news, and we want to say clearly where we stand.

## Meet our friends

We are not the only ones, and we are happy to point you to the others:

- **[Lombyte](https://github.com/mateuszklysz/Lombyte)**, by our friend
  [mateuszklysz](https://github.com/mateuszklysz). It is a byte-matching decompilation of the **NTSC-U**
  release of Ratchet & Clank, with the same aim as ours: readable C that compiles back to exactly the original
  bytes. Ours follows the **PAL** release, so between us both versions of the game are covered.
- **[ReRAC](https://github.com/re-rac/rerac)**, by [aslanhudajev](https://github.com/aslanhudajev). It
  takes a different road to the same game: a native PC port, written in Rust. Understanding the original code
  is what makes a project like that possible, so the work on all sides feeds into each other.

We are in touch with both and we are working together.

## The same goal, so why would we fight?

A decompilation is a huge job: thousands of functions, and every one of them has to come out as exactly the
same bytes as the original. Nobody finishes that alone, and nobody is competing for a prize. When another
project figures out a function, it is not a point lost for us. It is one more piece of the game that all of us
now understand.

So, to everyone who loves this game and this community:

- **Credit each other.** If you learned something from another project, say so, in the source and in the
  commit. Many of us already do, and it shows.
- **Share what you find.** A name, a structure or a compiler quirk helps everyone, whichever release or
  approach they work on.
- **Disagree kindly.** Projects will make different choices, and that is fine. Argue about the code, never
  about the person.
- **Welcome newcomers.** Every one of us started out not knowing how any of this works.

> "In a world where you can be anything, choose to be kind."
>
> — Nitya Prakash

Kindness is free, and it is the reason people stay around long enough to finish something this big.

## Want to join in?

Pick the project that fits you best: they all welcome help, and each has its own contributing guide. You
can also ask anything in our [Discord](https://discord.gg/Sfd2B54PDG), whichever one you end up helping.
